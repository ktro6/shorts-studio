Australia sent machine guns to fight twenty thousand emus. The soldiers gave up. It starts in 1932, in Western Australia's wheatbelt, full of veterans given land after the war. The wheat is their whole life. Then twenty thousand emus walk out of the desert and eat it. The farmers beg for help. The government sends two soldiers, two machine guns, and ten thousand rounds. The newspapers laugh. 'Two machine guns for chickens!' The army expects this to take a week. Wait, how does a bird outgun the army? You see, a machine gun is built for a crowd that stands still. Emus do not stand still. They run at fifty kilometres an hour, split into tiny groups, and scatter like popcorn across a hot pan. Some keep running after they are hit. So the army packs up and leaves. The government sends them back, and they kill nine hundred and eighty-six emus with nine thousand eight hundred and sixty bullets. Ten bullets for one bird. Then the government tries a bounty, and a fence. Farmers do in months what the army cannot do in weeks. The army never came back. The emu is on Australia's coat of arms. It has been there the whole time.
